    Chapter 7 - Session 6

    Eight Brave Souls and the Endless Stair

    Events and Actions

    The night was still young after the Mondo Ruka had been driven from Barrows's hilltop fort, and the screams in the jungle had died away. The party took stock. The natives had chosen around six sacrifices, more than usual, perhaps to make up for the fight at the cove, and Shaboo, the man the party had saved from the shackles, was sure his own people would kill him for escaping his fate. Barrows explained that most of the surface natives were resigned to the sacrifices as the price of living on the island: they had little power over what came up from below, and nowhere to go. As far as he could tell, they had never known any other life, and it was only at night during the full moon that the island was truly dangerous. One suggestion was to put Shaboo to sleep and chain him back to the wall; another was to hide him, perhaps disguised as one of the party. The talk moved on before either was settled.

    Talk turned to the origins of the island's ruins. Someone remarked that the Divinity of Two teaches that a child born without the Church's blessing will be deformed. Such babies are said to be killed at birth in the North, and the Church claims they were once far more common, before people came into its care. The Ruka, living where the Church had never been, fit that story uncomfortably well. Asked about markings on the ruins, Captain Barrows said they were massive stonework, comparable only to the great works of the Church or the Crown in the North, and would have demanded an immense communal effort. He had seen geometric patterns, probably decorative, and a few tablets or signs bearing writing he did not recognize, perhaps that of the islanders' ancestors, but nothing resembling the Church's symbols. He had never thought to ask the natives about them. The bird riders, he added, were no more likely to be the builders than the surface natives. They were not sophisticated builders, were not threatened by the Ruka, lived off the sea with their birds, and could leave whenever they liked.

    Randolph the Chronicler explained that his profession gathers new information rather than studying old, and that others at the Academy, such as the contact he had once named to the party, would be better placed to answer. He did know tales of great builders of the past, and said it was believed that the Capitol itself was built over such ruins, long since built over many times. His own guess was that other people lived in these lands before the present peoples, though where they went, or whether "we are them," he could not say; preventing the loss of such knowledge was the very reason his people existed. Cosimo gave the Church's account of history: before the Two there was nothing but false gods, barbarism, and "hokum," and civilization came only through the Church's teaching and its partnership with the kings. When the party pressed the point that their own abilities did not come from the Two, Cosimo clarified that the Church does not teach that other powers once existed and died out, only that all other worship was false and the Two the only true gods. Asked whether the ruins resembled the red pillars beneath the Crow's Nest, he said he would need daylight to judge, and the party resolved to examine the ruins more closely then.

    Barrows said he had never seen the Ruka come up two nights running, though they had never before been denied a sacrifice or thrown back so hard. He was worried about the coming night, though not the one after. Smaller groups had occasionally come up at night outside the full moon. The party noted that the light had genuinely afflicted the Ruka rather than merely frightening them, yet they could plainly function in dim light, by full moon or in fog, and someone wondered whether they actually needed a little light, since they came under the full moon rather than on moonless nights. Others cautioned that the night's success owed much to surprise, and that the Ruka might bring powers of their own to bear if they returned. Barrows then described his own descent. He had gone down by day with torches, which proved sufficient; it was the creatures, not the darkness, that defeated him. They chased him out, and he believed only the daylight at the top had stopped their pursuit. He had not known then what the creatures were, and only later learned the islanders' name for them. The natives had refused to go with him, and the few men who returned had no wish to go back; he would not order them, since he could no longer lead them. Afterward he had sealed the staircase with gunpowder, which he now regretted as a waste of precious powder, since he had not known then that there were other ways in. He knew of two spiral staircases, the sealed one and another still open, and of a low, wide crack beyond another field of jars, from which he suspected the creatures came; the Ruka evidently also had a way out near the wreck of the Advance. There might, he admitted, be hundreds more. Deeper down, he said, the stair's spiral grew immense, the largest he had ever seen, broken by landings with worked passages leading off. Below, those worked passages gave way to natural ones, some wide enough for four or five abreast, others so narrow they had to go single file, and he expected there were places where climbing would be needed. He had used his chip much like a compass needle, passing side openings because it drew him deeper. Mani, by contrast, had only ever used his totem sense to choose a heading at sea or across country, though it had once guided the party through the fog in the land of the little folk; he warned the others not to count on it in a labyrinth. Light from above had faded a little way down the stair, and beyond that there was none at all. In the great cavern where he was ambushed he had seen no torch or lantern, only reflections off the stone and confused flashes of light amid the chaos.

    The party then tested Barrows's black stone chip and their own sliver. The two fragments took no notice of each other, but both stuck to a Medallion like iron to a lodestone, and Barrows found they would not stick to any other metal. When Mani took his own Medallion off, tucking it into his powder-stained white hat for safekeeping, and tried again, it became clear that the Medallion, not the fragments, gave him his sense of the totem. Barrows insisted he wore no hidden Medallion, and he heard nothing when one was jingled. With Randolph excluded, the party told him how they had come by the Medallions: a hole behind the cellar wall of the Crow's Nest, a skeleton wearing a Medallion, and each of them going in and coming out with one. Barrows confessed himself confused and amazed but not disbelieving; he had seen their light, and he knew there was more to the world than was openly discussed, which was why he was here, and what the Triad was working toward. Mani explained that only he could remove his own Medallion; the others' attempts to take theirs off ended with them distractedly wandering away. He described how its single cut had multiplied after the Divinity of Two destroyed the earth totem on Leela's island. Barrows felt the invisible Medallion on another wearer's neck and was amazed. Mani refused to let him hold or put on his own, his hand going to his sword, and warned that it had harmed others who had tried. Asked what the Medallions were, the party admitted they did not know whom to ask; even the little folk of the swamp had been mysterious on the subject. They added that they believed the Church knew of the Medallions and that this was one reason for the heresy declaration. Cosimo said frankly that the powers of the Church were not what the party could do, and that he had not yet decided whether his own power came from the Two.

    The question became when and how to go below. All agreed the party would go down, and that time was the one currency they could spend only once. Barrows warned that going underground was very dangerous; he had gone down with about twenty men and come out with six. The party weighed a large group against a small one, how much light they could make between them, and whether to haul more powder up from the Advance, both for explosives and to scatter on the ground for smoke that might reveal hidden entrances. Werner dropped the idea once he realized he still had his keg. Cosimo's light could be sustained for hours, but only at the cost of nearly everything else, and Noddy's night vision worked only on the caster. They took stock of their kit: a swivel gun, more than a hundred feet of rope including spider silk rope, pitons, a hatchet, the keg, a kettle and cooking supplies, sail cloth, the Father's Ember, which glowed faintly enough to be seen at a distance and could light a fire, a case of grenades, red, green and yellow signal flares, and torches. Some argued for going down that very day, while the Ruka might be on the surface, perhaps waiting in a dead-end side passage near the entrance for them to pass; others wanted to stay and protect Barrows's people from a second attack. In the end the party decided to send Barrows, his sailors, and Shaboo off the island to the party's ship. Barrows warned that if he took his men aboard, they would never willingly come back; the party replied that they hoped he would not need to, if they found his captain. He hesitated, looking at his withered leg, then admitted that he would only be a burden below. When he put it to his crew, they were ecstatic at the chance to escape, and he agreed to await the party's return aboard the Medallion. Salter, the bosun's mate who had come from the Advance and knew the route and the gear left at the cliff, would guide them down to the boats and vouch for them with a note to the sailing master. He judged the descent would be hard on Barrows but possible, and that one boat, perhaps making two trips, would serve. This would also remove Shaboo before his people woke. The party chose to clear Barrows's sealed staircase, the closest entrance and presumably one the Ruka no longer used.

    Before Barrows left, the party prepared him for the ship he was about to board. The Medallion belonged to no nation, and Nicolas, whom Barrows remembered as a fervent junior priest, was aboard. He was no longer a follower of the Two but of "the Three." Cosimo had once preached that his new powers came by special grace of the Two, and had attributed some of them to Handsome Gulliver Gold, conjuring a moving, speaking image of him; Nicolas had taken it as a sign of a mother, a father, and perhaps a son. Barrows was asked not to disabuse him, and warned that the rest of the crew did not know everything the party had just told him. The party explained that they had been excommunicated, had taken up a life of "spicy adventure," and had fought off Northern ships. They showed him the heresy poster, though they could not recall whether his own name was on it, and he observed drily that his return to the Navy now seemed less likely. They also warned him that their ship had eyes that looked at people; he resolved not to mention that to his crew until they were in the boat. The news of an uneasy truce between the North and the South was new to him. The party decided the ship should hold its station off the island rather than leave to take on water, which it had already done when they visited the elves watching the island, so that it would be there if they needed to flee. Barrows described the location of every entrance he knew; the crack was the farthest, and the sealed staircase the closest. He then recounted his route below as best he could. He had gone down the staircase, passed a flat landing with three passages, and continued to a large worked room. He had crossed the room straight from where the stair entered and taken a passage on the far side that became natural, twisting, and generally descending. That passage led to a great cavern of stalactites and stalagmites, where he was attacked partway across. His leg had been afflicted not by a weapon but by a blow from one of the creatures, perhaps one of their leaders, though in the darkness and mayhem he could not be sure. Mani borrowed Barrows's chip for the expedition. Cosimo made sure they carried ropes of several lengths and thicknesses for rigging, though none of the spider silk went with Barrows's party, and each of the party took a couple of days' food and a pair of water skins.

    At around four in the morning, after only about four hours of poor sleep, the two groups set out separately. Barrows's men, who had not slept at all, nearly had to carry him; they were eager to be off the island by dinner, though uneasy about passing again through the place where they had been attacked. Barrows advised the party to avoid the natives, who were always most difficult just before and after a sacrifice, resentful that his crew refused every lottery, and who would certainly not help them. The eight who went below were Mani, Werner, Riu, Noddy, Knick Nab, Cosimo, Leela, and Randolph. Gus and the sailors who had helped rig the cliff remained with the shore party. After about an hour of hacking along overgrown paths, they reached a ruin on a small rise. It was more complete than most, with part of a domed roof still standing, built from huge, skillfully worked stone blocks, and birds flapped among its broken walls. Cosimo was amazed the partial dome still stood and judged the building highly sophisticated and nothing like Northern style, though it had none of the red clay brick of the Crow's Nest pillars. So much stone must have been brought from elsewhere, he noted, which implied a large, organized society, yet he saw no sign of the roads he would have expected, unless the jungle had swallowed them. Inside, up a broad flight of overgrown stairs, was a raised circular platform ringed by steps and buried under rubble that had clearly fallen far more recently than the surrounding ruin. Knick Nab and Cosimo organized the work while Mani stripped off his shirt and vest, and Cosimo built a tripod crane from three trunks with a block and tackle. None of the stones needed breaking; the blocks were shifted just a few feet aside to open the way, since stacking them to be dropped back later would have taken far longer. After about four hours they uncovered the top of a spiral staircase, and after two more the opening was just large enough to squeeze through: a clear choke point, easy to block from either side. It was around midday. Exhausted, the party rested for four hours instead of widening the hole, leaving the crane rigged for a quick escape, and gathered the pitch torches Barrows's sailors had made. They squeezed in one at a time in the late afternoon, passing their gear down after them.

    The staircase was carved directly out of the rock, about two people wide, with no railing and no central pillar, only an open shaft. Looking down it, they could make out a turn or two of the spiral and then only empty shaft, its bottom unseen even a hundred feet down; the crane above, they noted, could be swung over the opening if needed. The steps were generous, a little larger than ordinary stairs, and there were no landings to break the descent. Something dislodged by one of the party tinked off the sides and vanished. Near the top, vines and creepers snaked across the steps and down into the shaft, making the footing slippery, but they soon gave way to bare, worn stone. Knick Nab led, his night vision turning the dim light to near daylight, followed by Riu with a torch, then Mani with his main gauche and pistol, Randolph with a small pistol, Noddy with Pocket on his shoulder, Leela with her great axe, and Werner with a torch at the rear, his view mostly blocked by Leela's axe; Cosimo walked in the middle. Werner's musket was of little use in so confined a space. Before long the opening above was only a point of light, and the two torches lit no more than thirty feet. Cosimo marveled the whole way down that no Northern mason could cut such a stair. The party whispered, called out loose rubble and pushed it against the wall rather than into the shaft, and, checking the walls and risers for markings, found only old scuffs, scratches, and stains. After about half an hour of slow descent they reached a flat landing with three doorways leading off in different directions, just as Barrows had described. Leela found no recent tracks there; the dust had been disturbed, but not for a long time. On the inside of the door frames were angular markings that looked like writing. No one could read them, but they were very reminiscent of the script on the party's old Gildenhome Map, raising the thought that the elves might once have been the builders. They were sketched into a notebook, though not in full, and Randolph, too, took careful notes.

    Holding both fragments, Mani attempted his totem sense and felt a very vivid certainty that a totem lay down the stairs below them. At the same moment, he became aware not only of the five Medallion wearers around him but of two other Medallion wearers, very far away. For a brief moment, the surface thoughts of those nearby rushed into his head. Riu was wondering how much deeper it went. Leela was firmly resolved that Batu Batu was her man, not someone else. Randolph was torn between excitement and despair, sure that what he was recording would make him renowned among his people yet fearing it would never reach home. Then the sensation was gone. Mani told the others only that they were heading in the right direction.

    During a short rest, some of the party sat on the steps with their feet dangling over the shaft, and someone wondered how so much unsupported stone could bear its own weight. Riu walked about two minutes down one of the level side passages while three companions watched his torch recede. The passage ran straight, but its stone gradually gave way to earth, as though tunneled through soil, and the air, dry until then, grew musty and damp. He fixed the smell and feel of it in his memory, turned back, and reported, and the party continued down the main stair. After a similar distance, one whole side of the staircase opened onto a large worked chamber, bigger than their torchlight could reach, while the stair continued downward. Mani felt strongly that they should not go deeper but straight across, as Barrows had done, and said so. Unlike the cavern Barrows had described, this room showed no reflections off its walls. The party extinguished the torches in favor of two directed spell lights, one near the front and one at the rear, and agreed that Cosimo would flood the chamber with light at the first sign of hostility. Picking their way across rubble and muck that would have tripped anyone walking in the dark, they came upon a mass in the middle of the room. It was a ring of worked stone, low enough to climb over, too large to be a totem, and raised slightly on little legs so that there was a gap beneath it. About ten feet across inside, it had a single gap through which one could walk in, and bore no markings, if it ever had. Knick Nab stepped into the middle of the ring. The party listened intently and heard nothing at all.

    NPCs & Creatures

    • Captain Barrows — shared what he knew of the ruins, the Ruka's habits, the island's entrances, and his route below, and was told the story of the party's Medallions, the state of their ship, and the truce. Accepting that he would be a burden underground, he left before dawn with his men and Shaboo to await the party aboard the Medallion, lending Mani his black stone chip.
    • Barrows's sailors — the surviving crew of the Advance at the fort; ecstatic to leave the island, they set off before dawn carrying Barrows. They had made a supply of pitch torches.
    • Shaboo — the rescued sacrifice, still fearing his own people. He was sent off the island with Barrows's party.
    • Gus — the party's marine, left with the shore party at the cliff.
    • Mathias, Jacopa, Radult, and Keyes — the sailors who helped build the rigging at the cliff, Mathias having returned to the ship with Old Salter to fetch the other three; all remained with the shore party.
    • Salter — the Medallion's bosun's mate, formerly of the Advance (not Old Salter, who had already been sent back to the ship); sent to guide Barrows's party down the cliff to the boats and to vouch for them aboard the Medallion with a note to the sailing master.
    • Randolph the Chronicler — excluded from the party's account of the Medallions; explained that Chroniclers gather new information rather than study the past, shared tales of ancient builders beneath the Capitol, and accompanied the party below, taking copious notes.
    • Leela — accompanied the party below with her great axe; found no recent tracks at the first landing.
    • Pocket — Noddy's seagull familiar, riding on his shoulder down the staircase.
    • The Mondo Ruka — not encountered this session. Barrows has never seen them emerge two nights running, though they have never before been denied a sacrifice.
    • The hilltop natives — expected to shun the party and refuse any help after the denied sacrifice; the party was advised to avoid them.
    • The bird riders — who call themselves the Sawl, according to Barrows; they live off the sea with their birds, apparently where the Ruka cannot reach them, and could leave the island.
    • Captain Maynard — still the object of Barrows's hope, though Barrows is now less sure his chip was leading him to Maynard rather than to the totem.
    • Nicolas — described to Barrows as aboard the Medallion and now a follower of "the Three."
    • Handsome Gulliver Gold — explained to Barrows as the origin of Nicolas's new beliefs.
    • Batu Batu — glimpsed in Leela's thoughts.
    • Stick Stab and Hans — the party's speculation as to the two distant Medallion wearers Mani sensed.

    Locations

    • Birdcrown Isle — covered with ancient, sophisticated ruins whose builders are unknown and which bear writing no one recognizes. Barrows knows of at least three entrances below: two spiral staircases, one of which he sealed, and a low, wide crack beyond a field of jars.
    • Barrows's fort — abandoned before dawn as Barrows and his men left for the party's ship.
    • The domed ruin — about an hour's walk from the fort, on a small rise; a more complete ruin with part of a domed roof, built of huge worked stone blocks. Inside, a broad stair leads to a raised circular platform where Barrows's sealed staircase lies. The party reopened it, leaving a tripod crane rigged at the narrow entrance.
    • The endless stair — a spiral staircase cut from the living rock, about two people wide, with no railing and an open central shaft of unknown depth. According to Barrows, its spiral grows far larger further down.
    • The landing of three passages — a flat landing about half an hour down, with three level doorways leading off in different directions; their frames bear angular writing reminiscent of the Gildenhome Map.
    • The earthen passage — one of the three side passages, running straight and level as its stone gives way to damp, musty earth.
    • The worked chamber — a large constructed room opening off the stair further down, littered with rubble and muck, larger than torchlight can reach. A raised stone ring about ten feet across stands in it on little legs, with a single entrance gap and no markings.

    Conflicts

    There was no combat this session. The party avoided fatigue penalties by resting four hours after clearing the entrance.

    Learnings and Discussions

    The island's ruins were built by an unknown, highly organized civilization, and their stonework rivals or exceeds anything the North can achieve. The writing at the first landing resembles the script on the Gildenhome Map, hinting that the elves may once have been the builders. Randolph believes the Capitol is built on similar ancient ruins. The Church teaches that before the Two there was only barbarism and false worship, that the Two are the only true gods, and that children born outside its blessing will be deformed.

    The Ruka are genuinely afflicted by bright light, yet they operate in dim light and may even need a little of it, since they emerge under the full moon. The black stone fragments stick only to the Medallions, not to each other or to other metal, and the Medallion, not the fragments, is what gives Mani his totem sense. Below ground, that sense was vivid: the totem lies further down. Mani also sensed two distant Medallion wearers besides the party and briefly heard the surface thoughts of those around him, though he kept this to himself.

    Barrows, who counts himself part of the Triad's cause, learned of the Medallions, the party's excommunication, the truce between the North and the South, Nicolas's new faith, and the strange nature of the Medallion, the ship. He and his crew were evacuated to the ship with Shaboo. The party chose the sealed staircase as the entrance least likely to be in use, and followed Barrows's route: past the landing of three passages, then straight across the worked chamber toward the passage that leads to the cavern where he was ambushed.

    Items Gained

    • Barrows's black stone chip, lent to Mani.
    • A supply of pitch torches from Barrows's sailors.

    Items Lost

    None. The tripod crane and block and tackle were left rigged at the staircase entrance.
