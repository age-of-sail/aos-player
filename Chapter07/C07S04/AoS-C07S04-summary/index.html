    Chapter 7 - Session 4

    A Meadow of Heads and a Captain Found

    Events and Actions

    With the fight on the beach over and the mist still hanging over the cove, the party took stock of the fallen attackers. Up close, the deformities looked natural rather than added on. One creature had a single eye in the middle of its forehead with no sign that any other eyes had been removed. Another had a third arm, smaller and misshapen, but matching the skin and tone of the other two. A two-headed brute was beyond anything a hat could hide. Two attackers were still alive. Mani pointed his sword at each in turn and demanded to know whether they spoke the party's language, but neither gave any answer he could understand: only feral snarls, with at most a faint hint of something like Islander. Mani had wanted to give them a chance to talk, but having seen no sign they were capable of it and judging them too dangerous to leave alive, he finished them both. He asked whether anyone wanted to try saving the rest of the dying; no one did, and the party made sure every one of them was dead. None of the party had been hurt. The spooky mist still hung over the cove, the water stayed suspiciously low for the time of day, and there was no sign of the horned old woman at the flap of vegetation high on the cliff.

    The search of the bodies turned up very little, and certainly nothing to suggest Captain Barrows; no one wore any officer's gear. The attackers carried primitive or obviously scavenged weapons and wore ragged, scavenged scraps of clothing. They had no food, no water skins, and no personal belongings, and all of them were wet and lean from swimming out of the cove. The one item that stood out was a plain sailor's working knife, out of place among their crude weapons. None of them had tattoos, but many were heavily scarred. Some of the scars looked like the result of rough living and wounds left untreated, while others, such as hash marks, looked deliberate and possibly ritual. Nothing was consistent across the group, and in particular there was no recurring pattern of three lines. At Mani's prompting, Randolph the Chronicler, who had been taking notes throughout, sketched the more unusual or deliberate-looking markings in his notebook.

    The apparent spellcaster who had hung back during the fight was examined more closely. It wore a large coat and a linen shirt rather than skins, filthy and perhaps years old, and around its neck hung the skull of some long-snouted animal. It carried a small curved dagger of bone or horn and a pouch of twigs and bones, none of it of any apparent significance. A single horn grew from one side of its head, with a topknot of hair on the other. At the party's request, Riu performed a rough examination with his medical knife. The horn grew straight out of the skull, and the skin around it was healthy and long healed, so the horn had not sprouted recently. Riu could not say whether the creature had been born that way or changed later. Apart from the horn, it was simply a large, flabby, corpulent figure. The question mattered to the party: if something on the island had turned these creatures into what they were, it might do the same to them.

    The party debated how to dispose of the bodies. Leaving them on the beach was rejected as unsanitary, since the cove might become the party's beachhead, and simply pushing them into the water risked having them float, draw the hammerhead and others, or wash back ashore. Blowing them up aboard the Advance was ruled out because the hulk might still be needed to carry survivors home. In the end Mani and Old Salter weighted the bodies with rocks stuffed into their clothing, rowed them out, and dropped them near the old mooring ring where the creatures had emerged, into water at least a hundred feet deep, where they sank out of sight. Mani sent each one down with a curse and a message for its friends about what happens to those who cross him. Meanwhile Noddy kept his spyglass on the cliff where the horned woman had vanished, but saw no one watching. Riu came down from his perch partway up the cliff, recovering the spider-silk rope and leaving his piton in the rock.

    The boat sent back to the Medallion for rope and more hands was about due but hadn't appeared. Noddy sent Pocket gliding down the channel to look for it. The water had dropped so far that a shoal of slimy rock now stood about two feet clear of the surface, cutting the channel in two; no boat could sail through it, let alone a ship. From the mouth of the cleft, Pocket could see the Medallion still riding offshore, with the boat bobbing alongside and people scurrying about on deck. About a hundred feet out from shore, Pocket also spotted three bodies floating in the water. They looked like more of the same misshapen creatures, not any of the party's own crew. Fearing that two or three sailors rowing back alone would be easy prey for whatever had come out of the water, the party wanted to warn the ship to stay away. Pocket couldn't safely fly as far as the ship to carry a note, and Randolph had no bird to send, so the party agreed on a quick round trip: all nine of them would row out, warn the ship, collect the rope, and come straight back. The boat could carry all nine of them but not the gear as well, and the party rejected shuttling it up the cliff into the unknown. Instead, they spent the better part of an hour moving everything brought ashore, including camping gear, casks of water and rum, a barrel of ship's biscuit, a keg of powder, a case of grenades, the kettle and cooking gear, and the swivel gun, up onto the Advance and down into her hold, where Noddy opened a temporary portal through the still-locked powder room door. Everything was stowed inside within the portal's few minutes, and the door was left as locked as it had been for years. By then it was about four in the afternoon and the mist was slowly lifting.

    The nine of them hauled the empty boat across the exposed shoal, and a mark was chipped into the rock at the lowest water line for future reference. On the way out they noticed something that made no sense: the water inside the channel was lower than the open sea, so they were rowing uphill to reach it. They passed the three floating bodies, none of them the horned spellcaster, and a medical look showed that at least one had been shot with a musket. At the Medallion, the quartermaster, Leopold, was shouting orders, and sailors with muskets were climbing down into the boat to go to the shore party's aid; on seeing the party arrive, he called them back aboard. Old Salter explained that on the row out, three or four of the same "abominations" had come up out of the water and tried to climb into the boat. He and Mathias had only just got away, and they had heard a great commotion back at the cove. Their report had caused some confusion aboard over what to do, and the rope already loaded for the shore party had been unloaded again. Nothing had attacked the ship itself, and no more time had passed aboard than the few hours the party had been away.

    The party agreed that the ship should stay well offshore and keep double watches against attack from below. The ship carried red, green, and yellow signal rockets, and the party settled on a simple flare code: green for "come and get us," and any other color for "come in force." Combinations of colors were rejected, since flares sometimes misfire or go off late. The flares would stay with the shore party rather than in the boats, in case a boat was taken. The returning group was the six PCs, Leela, Randolph, and Old Salter, who took Gus's place. They would take both boats, leaving the Medallion her third, along with three hundred more feet of rope, a grappling hook, and block and tackle. By the time everything was ready it was close to six, and the cleft would soon be dark. Mani was keen to go straight back and keep watch on the cove, the water, and the cliff overnight, but the others had no wish to spend a cold night on a monster-haunted beach at the bottom of a hole they couldn't climb out of in the dark, so the party slept aboard the Medallion.

    The party set out at first light, weapons loaded, under an ordinary morning mist that was already burning off. The three floating bodies were gone. The channel was at low tide, much as it had been when they left the day before, but this time there was no uphill row, since its level matched the open sea's. No moon was visible in the sky. Both boats, one of them loaded with gear, were hauled across the shoal again, which left everyone soaked and feeling badly exposed. The slime showed no trace of anyone dragging a boat over it, not even the party's own crossing the day before. Back at the cove, Noddy reopened the powder room so the stores could be recovered. Everything looked as the party had left it, except that the beach, which had been covered in footprints at the previous low tide, had been washed clean. The tide had evidently risen properly at some point overnight and gone back out.

    The ascent followed. A climber went ahead to drive pitons and fix rope at the hard spots, while Cosimo shaped one of his solid illusions into a floating disc. It moved at a walking pace, had to stay within about a hundred feet of him, and could carry about 250 pounds, roughly half again his own weight, for some twenty-seven minutes. By riding it himself with a modest load, he could cover the full three-hundred-foot cliff in about two minutes each way and ferry the party's gear up in about three trips. Most of the path was a narrow, easy stair, but there were three short, genuinely hard stretches. On the last of these, some two hundred feet up, a piton came loose under Mani, who was leading the way with others strung out below him, and he only just caught himself. The scare slowed him, and everyone behind him took the rest of the climb more cautiously. At the top, the party judged that while the climb was manageable with time and preparation, it would be deadly for anyone in a hurry or in fog, and there was no safe way down in an emergency for anyone who couldn't fly. They couldn't help imagining earlier climbers fleeing up it with those creatures behind them. Whoever had made the path climbable had evidently pulled their own ropes up behind them, and the party followed suit: the pitons stayed in place for a quick descent later, but Riu pulled the rope up so that nothing from below could follow.

    Ahead lay the meadow Noddy and Pocket had glimpsed before: a shallow valley ringed by jungle very like the flora of Leela's own island, its floor covered from the cliff edge to the far hills in hundreds upon hundreds of small clay pots laid out in regular concentric rings. Each pot had a wide base, a smaller bulbous top, and a wooden stopper. Some had toppled over, but none were visibly spilling anything, and the pots stopped at the valley's edge rather than climbing the surrounding hills. Noddy had half suspected a minefield laid to stop anyone climbing up from the cove, but a careful search found no tripwires, fuses, or anything else linking the pots together. Riu lifted one pot telekinetically, straining even at its few pounds, and dropped it from thirty feet, but it only thumped into the grass. He tried again from higher up, and this time the top broke away from the bottom, sloshing out a little liquid. Through his spyglass, Noddy could make out something roughly ball-shaped in the lower half. Riu tried and failed to pull the stopper from another pot by hand, then went to inspect the broken one. The top half was empty, and the bottom held wispy, hair-like material, which he left well alone. The slimy liquid that had sloshed out smelled horrible, and no one could identify it. When the broken pot was cracked fully open with a boot, it held a small, shriveled, gray human head with wisps of hair and recognizable features. The head was larger than the pot's neck, as though the pot had been built around it.

    Riu's closer medical examination confirmed the worst. It was a whole adult head, skull and all, somehow shrunken and compacted, and it had been crudely hacked from its body with no sign of careful work. It was not a baby's head, though it might conceivably have belonged to someone as small as the pygmies the party had once met. It bore no horns or other deformities beyond its impossible size. The features were too warped for anyone to be recognized, so there was no point opening every pot looking for lost crewmates. A tracking check showed the pots had been laid down over a long span, some recently, within weeks, and some many years ago. A newly placed pot, less weathered and less overgrown, held another shrunken head of the same size in clearer liquid. The pots bore no markings of any kind. There were far more of them than the Advance had ever had crew, and Randolph, sketching all the while, said he had never heard of any burial rite like it. Someone suggested it might simply be a local way of honoring the dead, but the party was in no mood for cultural sensitivity. Several vowed to find whoever was responsible and put an end to it, and everyone was eager to get out of the clearing, sure they were being watched. The discovery colored how they meant to approach whoever they met next.

    From above, Pocket confirmed that the Medallion was still offshore. Inland lay jungle and hills, with a larger mountain in the distance. The ring of regular, crenellation-like rock spires seen from the sea continued around the whole visible rim of the island. Deeper inland, several jungle-covered masses rose out of the canopy, looking more like overgrown structures than natural hills, but there was no smoke anywhere. The party agreed that its first priority was to learn the fate of Captain Barrows and the Advance's crew, with the island's totem a close second, and set off toward those masses. Rather than leave their stores behind, they cut two saplings with the hatchet and slung sailcloth between them as a litter for the casks and heavier gear, which a pair of bearers could drop in a hurry if a fight broke out. Leela and Noddy took the lead as trackers.

    The jungle was dense and loud with animal life, including the occasional roar or trumpeting call. Once under the trees, the party found narrow, single-file paths, some made by animals and some evidently not. Pocket could only rise above the canopy at occasional gaps. Leela remarked on how much it all resembled her home, though there was no sign of the great webs of Spider Island, and no giant birds had been seen since the meadow. Late in the afternoon, the party heard rhythmic chanting ahead, along with a scraping sound. Leela listened for a moment and pronounced it a work song, Islander in feel but not really about anything. The party's totem sense told them only that they were in the right place, the same thing they had felt from offshore.

    From cover, the party watched a group of Islanders working around an overgrown ruin of sculpted stone, with square-topped pillars far more sophisticated than anything else in the jungle. These Islanders were of ordinary size, wore none of the gold Leela's people had, and all carried spears or similar weapons, though they were busy working rather than standing guard. On the ruin's raised platform stood an enormous clay cauldron, big enough to boil a man in, but with no fire beneath it and no steam rising from it. Two Islanders worked at something just out of sight behind it and every so often tossed something in, while a third, at the foot of the steps, watched and gave instructions. Others sat about or chopped wood. Magically sharpened hearing picked up a dialect that was clearly Islander but not quite Leela's, and showed that the workers were counting aloud with each item they added: one hundred sixty-three, sixty-four, sixty-five. Noddy lit a slow match, and Cosimo offered to make the party look like Islanders, an offer that was declined. Then three rough-looking sailors in ordinary sailors' clothes, with no officers' uniforms among them, walked freely out from the front of the temple-like ruin, muttering among themselves. The Islanders showed no alarm. The overseer joined the sailors, and together they shouldered a large section of planking and carried it off toward a bigger hill.

    The party wanted to talk to the sailors and had no reason to take on the cauldron workers, so they skirted the ruin and followed. Cosimo wrapped everyone in a ten-foot sphere of silence, which kept the party huddled tightly together and made the jungle eerily quiet. Leela walked at the front with standing orders to step forward and greet anyone who spotted them as friends searching for their own people. Pushing through the undergrowth would have split the group, so they kept to the paths. Rounding a bend, the party came face to face with two Islanders, who turned and fled before Leela could say a word. Cosimo dropped the silence, and within half a minute an alarm of clanging began. While the others debated, Cosimo cast Facade on himself, taking on an Islander's appearance so as not to be the first target, and stepped aside into the jungle to shadow the group. The rest of the party moved forward into a large cleared area at the foot of a hill. The hilltop was crowned with ruins fortified by every kind of barrier imaginable: sharpened stakes and logs halfway up the slope, and barricades built from ship's timbers, jungle wood, and broken stone. The clanging came from up there, where Islanders with spears, clubs, and a couple of muskets stood alongside sailors armed with muskets and pistols. There was no sign of any cannon. At the foot of the hill, the party took cover in a smaller ruin with half-standing walls, where they found posts fitted with manacles. It offered decent shelter, though the defenders held the high ground. Old Salter gratefully set down the heavy swivel gun he had been carrying.

    The party hailed the hilltop with a shout of "Ahoy," and when the clanging stopped, a sailor called down in the party's own language to ask who they were. Choosing honesty, Mani answered that they were former crew of the Advance, shore crew on the island two or three years before, who had come looking for their crewmates. He swept off his hat in a bow, introduced himself as Manuel de Barret, and asked where Captain Barrows was. Werner and Riu named themselves as former Advance men as well, while Cosimo, still disguised as an Islander, kept quiet in the bushes. The announcement set off a commotion above, and some of the sailors ducked out of sight. A stare-down followed, with Werner leaning nonchalantly against a pillar, rifle cradled and ready. Mani called up that they had come a long way, that they had the means to take the castaways off the island, and that they could always leave if they weren't wanted. At last a figure pulled himself up behind the barricade. It was Captain Barrows, who asked whether they were "those young men." Mani reminded him that they had helped him when he was in the stocks, and Barrows, astonished and delighted, asked at once whether they had come on a ship and where it was. Told it was waiting offshore rather than in the cove with the Advance, he called it great news, if ill-timed, before allowing that any time was better than never. When Mani offered to come back another time, perhaps in five years, Barrows would not hear of it, insisting they come up at once and tell him how they had found him, while a section of the palisade was pulled aside to let them in. Mani attempted a triumphant strut up the hill, which ended in an undignified stumble despite the subtle illusory glow Cosimo cast around him.

    At the top, Barrows proved to be in bad shape. Three years older, he had a scraggly beard and a ragged, disheveled uniform, though he still carried himself like a proper officer. He moved about in a slung chair he could hop into and out of. One trouser leg was missing entirely, and the leg beneath was shriveled, blackened, and mottled, plainly very unhealthy, leaving him barely mobile, if at all. When someone wryly remarked that he looked well, Barrows replied that there was a story there. Asked what the impressive fortifications were meant to keep out, he said only that they would get to it.

    NPCs & Creatures

    • Captain Barrows — found alive at last, commanding a fortified hilltop camp of sailors and Islanders deep in Birdcrown Isle's jungle; unkempt, bearded, and badly lamed, with one leg shriveled, blackened, and mottled, moving about in a slung chair. Recognized the party as the young men who had saved him from the stocks, welcomed them in, and was eager to hear that their ship lay offshore, though he called the news ill-timed.
    • The abominations — the misshapen, mutated creatures that attacked from the cove, so called by Old Salter. On closer study their deformities looked natural rather than added on: extra limbs that matched the rest of the body, a single central eye, a second head. They were feral and showed no language anyone could understand, carried scavenged weapons and clothing, and had heavy scarring, some of it possibly ritual. A second group of three or four tried to climb into the boat carrying Old Salter and Mathias back to the Medallion; three bodies from that encounter, at least one shot with a musket, were seen floating offshore and were gone by the next morning.
    • The horned spellcaster — the abomination that hung back during the beach fight; examined after death. It wore a large coat and a linen shirt, a long-snouted animal skull on a cord, and a bone or horn dagger, and had a single natural, long-established horn growing from its skull beside a topknot of hair.
    • The horned old woman — not seen this session; the flap of vegetation hiding her refuge on the cliff showed no sign of her.
    • Randolph the Chronicler — sketched the creatures' scarring and the shrunken heads in his notebook, said he had never heard of a burial rite like the pot meadow, had no bird to send to the ship, and accompanied the party up the cliff and into the jungle.
    • Leela — tracked through the jungle alongside Noddy, remarked on its likeness to her home island, identified the Islanders' chanting as a work song, and walked at the front with orders to greet anyone who discovered the party.
    • Old Salter — reported the attack on his boat by three or four abominations on the row back to the Medallion; helped Mani sink the bodies; replaced Gus on the returning shore party and lugged the swivel gun through the jungle.
    • Mathias — escaped the attack on the boat alongside Old Salter.
    • Leopold — the Medallion's quartermaster; was ordering armed sailors into a boat to go to the shore party's aid when the party returned.
    • Gus — stayed aboard the Medallion, with Old Salter taking his place on the shore party.
    • Pocket — Noddy's familiar; scouted the channel and spotted the floating bodies, confirmed the Medallion's position from the clifftop, spotted the jungle-covered structures inland, and helped guide the party through gaps in the canopy.
    • The hammerhead shark — still in the cove; the party briefly considered leaving the bodies to it.
    • The cauldron Islanders — ordinary-sized Islanders without gold, speaking a dialect related to but distinct from Leela's. They were working at an overgrown stone ruin in the jungle, counting aloud as they dropped items into an unheated clay cauldron, and were apparently working alongside the stranded sailors.
    • The hilltop camp's sailors and Islanders — rough-looking sailors in ordinary sailors' clothes, armed with muskets and pistols, defending a fortified ruin alongside spear- and club-armed Islanders, a couple of whom carried muskets. Two Islanders who met the party on a jungle path fled and raised the alarm.

    Locations

    • The Advance's cove — the water stayed unnaturally low through the afternoon, low enough to expose a shoal of slimy rock that cuts the channel in two and forces boats to be hauled across by hand. The party chipped a mark into the rock at the lowest water line. The next morning the channel's level matched the open sea, and the beach had been washed clean of footprints.
    • The Advance — her still-locked powder room held the party's shore stores overnight, put there and later recovered through portals Noddy opened in the door.
    • The cliff path — the three-hundred-foot climb from the cove to the island's upper rim; mostly a narrow, easy stair, but with three short, dangerous stretches. The party left pitons in place and took the rope up with them.
    • The meadow of pots — the shallow valley above the cliff, covered in hundreds upon hundreds of small, stoppered, unmarked clay pots set out in concentric rings, each holding a shrunken human head in liquid. Some pots have been there for many years and others only weeks.
    • The island's interior — dense jungle very like Spider Island's flora, crossed by narrow single-file paths, with hills, a larger mountain in the distance, and jungle-covered masses that look like overgrown structures. The crenellation-like rock spires seen from the sea run all around the rim.
    • The cauldron ruin — an overgrown ruin of sculpted stone with square-topped pillars, rising out of the jungle, where Islanders were working around a giant clay cauldron.
    • The fortified hill — a large hill at the edge of a cleared area, crowned by ruins fortified with stakes, logs, and barricades of ship's timbers, jungle wood, and broken stone; Captain Barrows's camp. At its foot stands a smaller ruin with half-standing walls and posts fitted with manacles.

    Conflicts

    No fighting this session beyond the aftermath of the beach battle. Mani killed the two surviving abominations once it was clear they couldn't or wouldn't communicate, and the rest of the dying were finished off. A piton gave way under Mani during the cliff climb some two hundred feet up, but he caught himself without injury. The party also learned that the boat carrying Old Salter and Mathias had been attacked by three or four abominations on the way back to the Medallion and only just escaped. The party's arrival at Barrows's fortified hill came close to a standoff, but it ended peacefully once Barrows recognized them.

    Learnings and Discussions

    The abominations' deformities appear natural and long-standing rather than recent or grafted, though the party couldn't tell whether they were born that way or changed later. Some of their scarring may be ritual, but there was no common pattern. The sailor's working knife among their weapons hints at contact with castaway sailors. The musket wound on one of the floating bodies suggests Old Salter and Mathias shot at their attackers while fighting them off, since the bodies lay too far from the Medallion to have been shot from her. The Advance would have carried some 120 to 140 men at full complement, far more than the dozen or so creatures the party fought, so the creatures alone couldn't account for her crew. The party also knows there are more of them than the beach attackers, since a separate group attacked the boat, and whatever lowered the water evidently survived the fight, so the threat remains. With no means of breathing water, the party has no way to follow the creatures to wherever they come from below.

    Someone evidently went to a great deal of trouble to make the cliff climbable and then made sure nothing could follow, which suggested to the party that survivors had fled upward and might be camped somewhere inland, away from the cleft.

    The cove's behavior remains unexplained. While the water was low, the channel sat lower than the open sea outside, so the party had to row uphill to leave it. By the next morning the level matched the sea, and the beach had been washed clean, so a real high tide had come and gone in between. The party suspects the attackers, or something below with them, can lower the water locally. The strangeness did not extend to time, however: the Medallion's crew had lived through the same few hours as the shore party.

    The pot meadow sits at the top of the only known route up from the cove. Its pots hold hundreds of shrunken human heads, laid down over many years and still being added to. No one, not even Randolph, had heard of any rite like it. Whoever is responsible is still doing it, and the discovery left the party wary of whoever they would meet next. The party agreed its first goal on Birdcrown Isle is to learn the fate of Captain Barrows and the Advance's crew, with the island's totem second; totem sense confirms one is present, but gives no clearer direction.

    Arrangements with the Medallion: the ship stays well offshore and keeps double watches against attack from the water. A green flare from the shore party means "come and get us," and any other color means "come in force." The code was kept deliberately simple, since flares can misfire or fire late. The shore party keeps the flares with it.

    Sailors are living alongside Islanders on the island, and Captain Barrows is alive, holding a heavily fortified ruin against some threat he has yet to explain. He was delighted that a ship had come, but called its arrival ill-timed.

    Items Gained

    • 300 feet of additional rope, a grappling hook, and block and tackle, brought from the Medallion.
    • Signal flares (green, red, and yellow), carried by the shore party.
    • A makeshift litter of two saplings and sailcloth, built to carry the shore party's stores through the jungle.

    Items Lost

    None. The pitons were left in place on the cliff path.
