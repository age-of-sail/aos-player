    Chapter 7 - Session 5

    Mother Moon and the Missing Sacrifice

    Events and Actions

    It was mid-to-late afternoon on the same day the party had climbed out of the cleft when they sat down with Captain Barrows inside his fortified hilltop ruin. Barrows looked much as they remembered him, if worse for wear after at least two years marooned. One leg was blackened and withered, and he could not walk unaided, getting about on a crutch or in a sling contraption in which his men could carry him. The ruin was in decent repair, with a serviceable roof and weather patching, and the slopes bristled with outward-pointing stakes meant to stop an attack on foot. The surrounding area was strewn with ruins of remarkable stonework, carved columns and great emblems, far beyond anything the island's natives seemed capable of. No giant birds had been seen since the party left the cleft.

    Barrows explained that he had never been able to leave because his ship was trapped. He had judged the channel a safe harbor and brought the Advance in, only to find it was a trap, and he had tried to leave a warning for anyone who came after. When the party described how the waters had dropped behind them in the cove, he said the creatures of the island had some command over the waters. He said there was much that was unnatural about the island, which was why Maynard had been sent there, why he himself had been sent, and why the Divinity of Two had taken an interest as well. Two priests had sailed on the original voyage, and one had gone ashore with Captain Maynard. The natives had told Barrows that Maynard, several sailors, and a priest went underground three or four years ago and never came back.

    Barrows admitted he regretted not taking the party fully into his confidence when he first brought them aboard; he had thought them more trustworthy than most and had meant to draw them in over time. Maynard had carried papers and maps indicating the island existed, and someone in the Navy had quietly secured him the mission to find it. Barrows had learned this only afterwards, from papers Maynard had given him with strict instructions not to open them until later. He spoke guardedly of an organization that believes the Church's account of how people must live cannot be the whole truth. The Church, he said, was afraid of the island, though he could not say why, since as far as he knew it held nothing of value. When the party asked about a large black stone, Barrows said he had never seen one, but the natives spoke of such a thing underground and believed it was what the Ruka worshipped. He assumed that was what Maynard had gone below to find.

    Asked about the heads in jars, Barrows said they were the work of the creatures the natives call the Mondo Ruka. Leela rendered the name as "the Blackheads." These were the misshapen attackers from the cove, and Barrows blamed them for the trap and for all sorts of evil on the island. There were hundreds of them. They lived underground and came out only occasionally to take people below, returning with heads; Barrows called them cannibals, and Leela confirmed that some islanders did practice cannibalism. The islanders sheltering near the fort were the Ruka's prey. Another group of islanders commanded the giant birds, and Barrows was sure they could have given him a way off the island. When he first arrived, however, some of his men had shot at them, killing a rider and a bird, and every attempt at a parley since had failed.

    The Ruka came out at night, mostly, and only when the moon was close to full. From their chanting and screaming, Barrows was convinced they worshipped the moon. When the party pointed out that they had been attacked by day, it was noted that the cove had been thick with fog at the time. Barrows expected an attack that night or the next, since the moon was nearly full, and he warned that the party's slaughter on the beach might have made the Ruka angrier than usual. The natives' answer was appeasement: they chose sacrifices by lot and left them shackled at sites around the jungle. What the party had seen at the cauldron was the lottery being prepared. Everyone took part, with no exceptions for age, sex, or rank, and Barrows had seen the chief and the chief's daughter draw from the pot. Most islanders were resigned to it, and anyone who balked after being chosen was outnumbered by those who had not been. Barrows and his men refused to take part, and the natives' cooperation with them rose and fell with the cycle of the moon. The less the Ruka were appeased, he said, the more forceful their next coming.

    Barrows knew of four ways underground and suspected there were many more, since the Ruka came from every direction. One was below the cove where the Advance lay, which the party confirmed: there was an opening underwater beneath the old mooring ring, about fifty yards from the mound where Barrows had buried some of his people. The second was a crack in a low rise at the far end of another large clearing of head-filled pots, about three hours' march inland. The third was an ancient staircase in the ruins. Barrows had gone some way down it, then sealed it with charges about a year ago, and had not been back since; knowing now how many other ways there were, he doubted that had been wise. The fourth was another staircase, perhaps half an hour away, that he had descended with a party of his crew. He had found a massive cavern and heard the howls of what sounded like hundreds of Ruka, and he had escaped, but many of his men had died there, along with his first lieutenant. That staircase was still open. He had thought of ambushing the Ruka as they emerged, but with too few men to cover even the known entrances he feared being surrounded in the jungle, and the hilltop was the best position he had found.

    The staircase expedition was also where he had been lamed. Barrows said most of the Ruka were feral, but some were leaders with other powers, and one of these had struck him with a bone staff. When the party said they had faced such a leader and been lucky, he told them they had been very good. Barrows remembered Riu as a surgeon's mate, and Riu examined the leg. It was shrunken, withered, and blackened, and its cracks were oozing pus. Barrows said the pain came and went and was worst when the moon was fullest. He had never had it amputated because there was no one he trusted to do it and he did not expect to survive. Riu told Barrows he had studied under Mistress Clara and Mistress Mary. Careful detection showed no active Essence, Mentalism, or Channeling at work, and a second, spell-aided diagnosis made it clear that this was a disease, not a curse, and one that was entirely foreign to him. Riu cast Poison/Disease Relief together with a flesh-wound healing spell. The pus dissolved away almost like mist, the lesions closed, and Barrows nearly screamed with joy as most of the pain left him. The leg itself remained withered and weak, and Riu judged he had brought a severe disease down to a moderate one.

    Three natives climbed the hill to ask the "sailor men" to take part in the choosing. Through one of his sailors who spoke Islander well, Barrows gave his usual answer: he and his men would not take part, but any native was welcome to shelter in the fort and help defend it, and he would do his best to rid the island of the Ruka. Both sides had clearly had this exchange many times before. When the envoys asked whether he spoke for the newcomers, Barrows said they were not his crew and could answer for themselves. Noddy stood by looking intimidating. The party refused too. The envoys insisted that all must draw from the cauldron, since all shared the same fate, and that in return they would supply food and help for the other twenty-eight days of the month. Asked how many Ruka to expect, they could only say "too many," more than they could handle; they had never been overrun only because they kept the Ruka appeased. When the party answered that they would find their own food and would stand on the wall to protect any who came, the envoys called them foolish. Refuse, and there would be no help at all, and the Ruka would keep coming, in greater numbers each time they went unappeased, until they came over the wall by the thousands.

    Barrows had brought three of the Advance's cannon to the fort, all he could haul through the jungle, though they could not be run out like a ship's guns. Each sat in a carriage backed by a mound of earth and staked down. He had powder and ball for about three shots apiece, and no grapeshot left. The powder was coarse, and he was reluctant to grind it down for the small arms. The party told him there was still powder aboard the Advance, enough to blow her up, though they meant to sail her home with him instead. It was too late in the day to fetch any. He thought the guns' greatest value lay in the noise, which had always driven the Ruka off before. He refused to fire gravel through them, since he expected to need them again. When the party suggested they could leave the next day, he said he was eager for the chance they had brought him but would not leave the island without learning his captain's fate. Mani told him the party wasn't leaving yet either, offering to let Barrows wait aboard their ship if need be: they had come a long way for answers.

    A suggestion to spend the remaining hours investigating the horned old woman in her cliff refuge, half a day away, was set aside in favor of preparing the hilltop. Barrows gladly accepted the party's help. His men had fortified the position as best they could, and before nightfall they would drive more stakes into the one easy path up the hill, though attackers on foot could work their way around such obstacles. The party talked through the night's defense. Barrows said the Ruka came as a disorganized melee horde, with only improvised missiles, and had never reached the top of the hill; gunfire and cannon had always broken them first. When the party promised him a great light in the sky courtesy of Handsome Gulliver Gold, Barrows asked who that might be and was told he would have to wait and see. He also warned the party not to trust Nicolas, the former junior priest of his ship, when they mentioned him. Knick Nab took charge of Barrows's sailors and set them to using the timber they had been hauling in to block gaps and improve firing positions. Cosimo set out his options: illusions, earth walls, light control, and sound control. The party's astronomy told them that the moon was waxing gibbous and would be full that night or the next. It would rise at about half past four, the sky would be clear, and it would set at around four in the morning. The ground offered no natural funnels, since the hill could be attacked from every side, though its base was open ground that no one could cross unseen. Beyond it the jungle was thick, damp, and anything but dry. The party agreed to keep an escape plan in reserve in case the Ruka came in overwhelming numbers.

    Meanwhile, the natives filed up to the cauldron at the nearby ruin to draw lots, while those trying to hide in the bushes were herded back to take their turn. Their priest announced that the Ruka had been fought by the newcomers, would be angry, and would need much appeasement, so many would be chosen, and the crowd did not look happy. Some who drew were plainly relieved, some less so, and every so often someone looked at what they had drawn in clear dismay and was quickly pulled aside. Werner went down with Leela as interpreter, his Twin-Faced Doubloon turned to help him persuade, while Cosimo used sound control to make his voice boom out over a hushed jungle. He told the natives they had lived all their lives in the shadow of the cauldron, that with the newcomers' help they could defeat the Ruka, and that they could push the cauldron "into the pits of yesterday's destiny." Leela paused midway to ask whether he really meant to say that, and the speech fell flat. One spearman began to drift toward the party, but someone growled at him and he shuffled back. Four people were chosen: one woman and three men. Three were led off in different directions into the jungle, and the fourth, a man, was manacled at the half-ruined wall the party had passed at the foot of the hill. The manacles were of foreign make, probably scavenged from a ship, and were fastened with pins placed just out of the captive's reach. The natives then scattered into the jungle.

    The party built bonfires at the four points of the compass around the hill. The swivel gun was given two improvised log cradles so it could fire from either position, with Old Salter assigned to it, and muskets were shared out, Noddy lending his. At about seven, with the light failing, the party went down to the shackled man. In Islander, they offered to free him if he would join and fight with them. Although he was terrified that his own people would kill him for escaping his fate, he agreed, on condition that the party protect him from his own people. His name was Shaboo. Asked about the other sacrifices, he pointed in three directions into the jungle, each perhaps a four-minute walk away. Cosimo left an illusion of Shaboo hanging slumped in the shackles, lasting about an hour and renewed as needed, and the real Shaboo was brought up the hill and handed a spear, though he spent the rest of the night curled up behind a wall. The fires were lit at about eight.

    At around ten, with the moon high and nearly full, howls rose from all around the jungle. There was a louder outcry in one direction followed by prolonged screaming that slowly faded, as if someone were being dragged away, and then the same thing came from another direction. Two Ruka appeared at the foot of the steps, paying the bonfire no mind, and stared up at the illusory Shaboo. One had three arms. They were gibbering at each other in something like Islander, and through careful listening the party picked out the word "missing" again and again. Both ran up and shook the shackles. Cosimo then flooded the whole south side of the slope with light as bright as day, stopping just short of the fort. The Ruka's screams changed tone at once; they covered their eyes and fled down the steps, and Werner shot one through the head, killing it instantly. The other escaped. A further scream from another direction seemed to account for the last of the four sacrifices. Reloading, the party reasoned that the Ruka, deceived by a decoy and with more of their number dead, would be angry and would take it out on the village, and that the best time to strike into their lair might be while they were abroad in force.

    The howling went on, then slowly fell into unison, a rhythmic chant shouted in what sounded like ecstasy from every side. Leela could not make it out, but one of the party could: "Mother Moon, Mother Moon." Barrows said they might chant like that for an hour, and that something might happen when it changed. Shaboo, asked what the Ruka would do, could only say they were not happy; his own people, he said, were hiding in the jungle, in their huts, and in holes, and he was sure they would kill him. Werner put down his rifle and struck up a sea shanty on his violin, which Cosimo backed with a grand, sweeping symphony conjured by sound control. The fiddling itself squeaked badly, and Barrows and his sailors were left dumbfounded by all of it. Mani and Riu went down to retrieve the dead Ruka while Cosimo's light still held. The stakes made the climb back far harder than the descent. The body snagged on one, and when they tried to free it, Mani was pulled onto the stake as well, though he was unhurt. In the end, Mani wrenched the stake out of the ground and the pair hauled the body up. It was an Islander except for a second face growing out of the side of its head, consistent with the creatures from the cove.

    Just as they reached the top, and as Cosimo's first light was expiring, the chanting broke apart and grew louder, and shadows began moving on every side. The Ruka rushed the hill from all directions at once. Riu loosed a burst of light on a cluster of them and threw a grenade, Knick Nab directed one of the cannon into the press, and the six sailors fired on Barrows's command. Cosimo threw a ring of daylight-bright light around the whole hilltop and well beyond it, keeping it dimmer inside the fort. Werner watched for a leader worth a shot but saw none and held his fire. The cannon liquefied several attackers. Between the sudden light, the smoke, and the ear-splitting din of musketry, cannon, and grenade, as loud as any sea battle, the rush broke almost at once, and in that light the party glimpsed contorted, multi-armed, twisted figures several ranks deep in every direction. Shaboo curled up even tighter.

    As the attackers fell back, screams of terror began to rise from the jungle, from places all around. It seemed the Ruka had turned on the hiding villagers instead. Barrows had never seen an attack on that scale or seen one broken so completely, and he could not understand where the light had come from when no flare had been fired. Cosimo could keep the light going, though at great cost to his strength, and the party chose to wait and see. They debated striking for an entrance to the tunnels at once, while the Ruka were out in force, but decided against it. Knick Nab counseled against recklessness, Barrows agreed, and setting out in the dark, through the horde, to a place none of them had been, without cannon, was judged too likely to end in disaster. Some of the screams ended abruptly and others faded into the distance, and by about one in the morning the chanting had died away and the ordinary sounds of the night jungle returned. The Ruka did not come back, and the party set watches.

    Barrows came over to say that the party had become very special since he last saw them, though that conversation could wait. The moon was not always at its peak in the depths of the night, and he suspected this had been an especially powerful night for the Ruka; the next might be worse still. He warned that morning might bring a new problem: the villagers always grumbled at his refusal to join the lottery and eventually came around, but they had never before had a sacrifice denied them, and he did not know how they would react. He approved of what the party had done all the same. The party pressed him on why he believed Maynard was still alive. Barrows said he had reason to believe it but was not sure he was willing to share it, and the talk grew heated. One of the party threatened to take both ships home, and Barrows insisted he was the senior captain present. The party told him they had been declared heretics and were effectively outside the Navy, and showed him the heresy declaration. Barrows replied that no one simply leaves the Navy, but the evidence of their shared condemnation removed his concerns. He reminded them that the organization behind the symbol, the Triad, survived only through secrecy. Even so, he wanted to know what they were, for their powers were nothing like the Two's, whose great power he had seen.

    The party moved away from the others for privacy, under a cone of silence from Cosimo. Randolph was excluded, and Barrows brought only his second lieutenant. Barrows explained that on the first voyage Maynard had guarded his navigation closely, even from his first lieutenant, and seemed to be following something. Barrows believed that something was like the small chip of black stone he now produced, which Maynard had left him with the vague instruction that it "will find him." Over more than a year it had nudged him in certain directions, roughly, inconsistently, and sometimes wrongly, until he found his way back to the island, despite the Shatters having changed the waters, though less so out here. Maynard had also given him papers he was forbidden to look at while the Divinity of Two might be near. Barrows believed the Divinity never learned of them, though he regretted having discussed Maynard's other papers with them. He had assumed it was leading him to Maynard, who must be somewhere underground. Pressed on how Maynard could still be alive after three and a half years, and why he had never come looking on so small an island, Barrows admitted he did not know for certain; he could only hope.

    In return, the party told him about totems. They explained how the Divinity of Two had used a Diad to destroy an air totem to cause the Great Change, enslaving Grantos in the process, and how they had since recovered him. They described the destruction of the earth totem on Leela's island, which created the Shatters, by a ship of the Divinity that had already failed once and lost a Diad, which the party recovered. They shared their speculation that the Two fear any power outside themselves, that the Divinity's own power seems tied to water, and that the totems may be elemental founts of fire, earth, air, and water, of which Barrows's chip was a fragment. They asked in turn what he could tell them of the Triad's leadership and beliefs, about which they knew almost nothing. When Barrows asked what power they had just displayed, and whether it came from his chip or theirs, they admitted they had guesses but did not fully understand it themselves. They were sure, though, that it did not come from the Two. One of them had at first believed his power came from the Two but no longer did. One of them could feel it most strongly near the totems, as if it came from the earth itself, and it felt different from the Two's power in a Diad. The party's totem sense placed this island's totem close by, but much more downward than outward. Barrows realized that what he had been sensing might be the totem rather than his captain. Borrowing the party's own sliver, one of the party found they could sense Barrows's chip nearby as well.

    NPCs & Creatures

    • Captain Barrows — the Advance's captain, stranded on Birdcrown Isle for about two years in a fortified hilltop ruin. Lamed by a disease inflicted by a Ruka leader's bone staff, which worsens with the full moon; he gets about on a crutch or carried in a sling. Riu relieved the lesions and most of the pain, but the leg remains withered. Refuses to join the sacrifice lottery and will not leave without learning Maynard's fate. A Triad member, he was guided back to the island by a black stone chip Maynard left him.
    • Captain Maynard — Barrows's former captain. Went underground on the island three or four years ago with several sailors and a priest and never returned. Barrows believes he is still alive below.
    • Barrows's second lieutenant — joined Barrows for the private discussion with the party.
    • Barrows's first lieutenant — killed, along with many of Barrows's men, on Barrows's expedition down the open staircase.
    • Barrows's sailors — six ragged sailors manning the fort, one of whom speaks Islander well and interpreted for Barrows. Organized by Knick Nab to strengthen the defenses and fought off the night's attack.
    • The Mondo Ruka ("the Blackheads") — the natives' name for the misshapen, cannibalistic creatures living beneath the island, the same as the abominations from the cove. They number in the hundreds, emerge mostly at night around the full moon, take sacrifices below, and return heads to the pot meadows. They chanted "Mother Moon" in unison before attacking, and were driven off by light, cannon, and musketry. Some of their leaders wield powers; one lamed Barrows with a bone staff.
    • The two-faced Ruka — one of two Ruka who came for the decoy sacrifice; shot dead by Werner. An Islander in every way except for a second face growing from the side of its head.
    • The three-armed Ruka — its companion, which shook the empty shackles crying "missing" and escaped when Cosimo's light flared.
    • Shaboo — a native man chosen by lot and manacled as a sacrifice. Freed by the party in exchange for their protection from his own people, he spent the night petrified and curled up behind a wall, convinced his own people would kill him.
    • The other three sacrifices — one woman and two men chosen alongside Shaboo and taken to separate sites in the jungle. All three were apparently carried off by the Ruka.
    • The hilltop natives — the islanders living around the fort, whom Barrows regards as capable and decent but desperate. They appease the Ruka through a lottery drawn from the cauldron, which includes even their chief and his daughter. They withdrew their offer of food and help after the party refused to take part. During the attack many hid in the jungle, where screams were heard after the Ruka were driven from the hill.
    • The natives' priest — presided over the lottery at the cauldron and announced that the Ruka would be angry and many must be chosen.
    • The three native envoys — came up the hill to demand that the sailors take part in the choosing, and called the party foolish for refusing.
    • The bird riders — islanders elsewhere on Birdcrown Isle who command the giant birds. They have refused all contact since Barrows's men killed one of them and a bird.
    • Leela — interpreted for the party, rendered "Mondo Ruka" as "the Blackheads," confirmed that cannibals exist among the islanders, and questioned the wording of Werner's speech midway through.
    • Randolph the Chronicler — stayed in the fort taking notes; excluded from the private conversation with Barrows.
    • Old Salter — assigned to the swivel gun.
    • Nicolas — mentioned to Barrows as having fallen out with the Divinity of Two; Barrows warned the party not to trust him.
    • Grantos — described to Barrows as the air-totem islander enslaved by the Divinity of Two and since recovered by the party.
    • Mistress Clara and Mistress Mary — named by Riu as his teachers in medicine.
    • The horned old woman — not seen; a proposal to investigate her cliff refuge was set aside.
    • Handsome Gulliver Gold — mentioned to Barrows as a possible light in the night sky; Barrows did not know who he was.

    Locations

    • Birdcrown Isle — strewn with ancient ruins of sophisticated stonework far beyond the natives' craft. The Mondo Ruka live in passages beneath it, and its totem lies somewhere below.
    • Barrows's fort — a ruin atop a large hill with a serviceable roof, ringed by stakes and barricades, with three of the Advance's cannon in earth-backed emplacements. The hill can be attacked from any side, but its base is open ground that cannot be approached unseen. The party added bonfires at the four compass points and improvised cradles for the swivel gun.
    • The sacrifice wall — the half-ruined wall with manacles at the foot of the fort's hill, where Shaboo was chained and Cosimo's illusion took his place.
    • The cauldron ruin — where the natives draw lots for the sacrifices.
    • The underwater entrance at the cove — an opening beneath the old mooring ring in the Advance's cove.
    • The second pot clearing — another clearing full of head-filled pots about three hours' march inland, with a crack in a low rise at its far end that Barrows believes leads below.
    • The sealed staircase — an ancient staircase in the ruins, a couple of hours from the fort, that descends into darkness. Barrows sealed it with charges about a year ago.
    • The open staircase — another ancient staircase, perhaps half an hour from the fort, by which Barrows descended to a massive cavern and lost many of his men. It remains open.

    Conflicts

    At around ten at night, two Ruka came to claim the decoy sacrifice and fled from Cosimo's sudden light. Werner killed one with a single shot to the head. Later, the Ruka rushed the fort from every side in great numbers, several ranks deep. The assault broke almost immediately under Cosimo's ring of light, Riu's burst of light and grenade, a cannon shot directed by Knick Nab, and volleys from Barrows's sailors, and the attackers turned instead on the villagers hiding in the jungle. None of the party or the fort's defenders were hurt. While Mani and Riu were hauling the dead Ruka up the hill, Mani was dragged onto a stake but escaped without injury. Riu's treatment greatly eased Barrows's diseased leg but did not cure it.

    Learnings and Discussions

    The Mondo Ruka are numerous, live underground, are most active at night near the full moon, and appear to worship the moon, chanting "Mother Moon" before attacking. They are strongly affected by sudden bright light, and fog is not part of their usual attacks inland. The natives say the Ruka also worship a black stone underground. The natives appease them with sacrifices chosen by lot, and the Ruka noticed at once when a sacrifice went missing. Denying them may anger both the Ruka and the natives, whose reaction in the morning is uncertain. Barrows believes the creatures have some command over the waters. His own leg is afflicted by a disease, not active magic, that worsens with the moon. The party's slaughter at the cove may have provoked the Ruka.

    Barrows knows of four ways below: under the cove, a crack at a second pot clearing three hours inland, a sealed staircase, and an open staircase about half an hour away. The party decided against a night sortie and agreed to reassess at daylight, when the Ruka should be back underground.

    Barrows confirmed that Maynard had papers and maps pointing to the island and that one of the Divinity's two priests went ashore with him. He revealed the black stone chip that guided him back to Birdcrown Isle. The party in turn shared its knowledge of totems, the Great Change, the Shatters, Grantos, and its speculation about elemental power and the Two's fear of it, and assured Barrows that their own powers are not of the Two. Totem sense places the island's totem below, and Barrows now suspects his chip may have been leading him to the totem rather than to Maynard.
