# Letter from Eleanor Mason

Among the personal effects looted from Captain Mason's sea chest during the escape from the
Fortnum was a small, oilskin-wrapped keepsake pouch: a plain gold ring and a handful of folded
letters in a woman's hand. This is the one letter from that packet worth reading in full.

> The Capitol, KC 8
>
> My dearest Herod,
>
> I pray this finds you well, and that the seas have been kinder to you than the winter has been
> to us here. I have news that I could not bear to entrust to anyone's pen but my own, though I
> have started this letter three times now and blotted the first two with foolish tears.
>
> You have a son.
>
> He came two nights ago, loud and furious about it, with your temper already plain in him if
> nothing else of you is yet visible in his face. He is small but sound, the midwife tells me,
> and feeds well, and I am mending faster than I did the first time, whatever that is worth to
> you from so far away. I have named him Herod, after his father, though I confess I already call
> him "little Herod" to keep us straight, and I suspect the house will call him that long after
> he has outgrown the need for it.
>
> I wish you could see him. I wish that more than I have wished for anything since you last sailed.
> He has your stubbornness already — he would not settle until I gave up and let him have his own
> way of it, which I am told is no way to raise a child and which I intend to do anyway, at least
> until you are home to tell me otherwise.
>
> The house is quiet without you in it, quieter still now that there are two of us waiting instead
> of one. I do not ask when you will be back — I have learned better than to ask, and I know you
> would tell me if you could — but I will say that I count the days regardless, and that your son
> will know your face from the miniature on the mantel long before he knows it from the man
> himself, unless the Two are kinder to us than they have been.
>
> Come home safe, Herod. Come home soon, if you can manage it, but safe above all. We will be here.
>
> Your loving wife,
> Eleanor
