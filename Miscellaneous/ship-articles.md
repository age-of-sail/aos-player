## Overview

Herein be a draft of the Articles of Agreement for the company of this vessel, offering sundry choices on matters of great import, drawn from the codes of privateers and King's ships alike. Let every man read, debate, and set his mark to the final laws ere we venture forth upon the seas.

---

## I. Command and Governance

### Option A (Democratic Rule)
1.  Every man shall have a vote in affairs of moment, whether it be the charting of our course or the choosing of a prize to be taken.
2.  The Captain's word is law in time of chase or battle. In all other matters, his authority shall be guided by the will of the company, as determined by a majority vote.
3.  The Quartermaster shall be the magistrate of the company, charged with settling all disputes and ensuring the voice of the crew is heard.

### Option B (Strict Hierarchy)
1.  The Captain's command shall be absolute, and all hands are strictly required to obey his orders as if they were writ by the King himself.
2.  He who disobeys a lawful command or presumes to strike his superior officer shall suffer the severest punishment the Captain deems fit.
3.  If any man has cause for complaint, he shall make it known to his superior officer in a quiet and decent manner, who will see that justice is done him.

---

## II. The Division of Spoils

### Option A (Pirate Shares)
1.  All prize, treasure, or other wealth lawfully taken shall be divided into shares. The Captain and Quartermaster to receive two full shares; the Master, Boatswain, and Gunner, one share and a half; other officers, one share and a quarter. Every common hand to receive one full share.
2.  Any man who should lose a limb or be grievously wounded in the service of the ship shall have compensation from the common stock ere the prize is divided. For the loss of a right arm, six hundred pieces of eight; for a left arm, five hundred pieces; for a right leg, five hundred pieces; for a left leg, four hundred pieces; for an eye or a finger, one hundred pieces of eight.
3.  He that first spies a sail which proves to be a prize shall have the finest pair of pistols aboard her, over and above his proper share.

### Option B (Merit-Based Shares)
1.  All prize and treasure shall be allotted into shares. A common seaman shall be granted one share. A man of special skill, such as a Surgeon or Master Gunner, shall be granted two shares. The Commanders of the vessel shall be granted three shares.
2.  An act of uncommon valour or singular skill in the heat of battle may be rewarded with an extra half-share, should the company vote it so.
3.  For grievous wounds suffered in service, compensation shall be provided from the common stock, the sum to be judged by the Captain and Quartermaster according to the severity of the hurt.

---

## III. Onboard Discipline

### Option A (Strict & Swift)
1.  No striking one another on board, but every man's quarrels to be ended on shore, at sword and pistol. The Quartermaster shall preside over these duels, which shall be to first blood.
2.  If any man shall be found guilty of stealing from a shipmate to the value of a single piece of eight, he shall be marooned upon a desolate shore with naught but a flask of water, a pistol, and one ball of shot.
3.  The lights and candles to be put out at the eighth bell. If any of the crew remain inclined for drinking, they are to do it on the open deck, under the stars.
4.  No person to game at cards or dice for coin of the realm.

### Option B (Orderly & Regulated)
1.  Let no man presume to strike his shipmate. All quarrels shall be brought before the Quartermaster, who shall mediate the dispute. If he cannot bring the parties to reconciliation, the Captain shall judge the matter and punish the offender.
2.  He that is found guilty of theft from a shipmate shall feel the lash and forfeit his share of the next prize taken.
3.  He who is guilty of drunkenness, if a seaman, shall be put in irons until he is sober. If an officer, he shall forfeit two days' pay for his lack of discipline.

---

## IV. Duties and Responsibilities

1.  Every man to keep his piece, pistols, and cutlass clean and fit for service.
2.  He that shall sleep upon his watch, or neglect his duty, or forsake his station, shall suffer a lashing or forfeit his share, as the company shall see fit.
3.  No man shall give or dispose of the ship's provisions, but every one shall have his equal share of fresh victuals and strong liquors.
4.  No snapping of guns or smoking of tobacco in the hold.

---

## V. Conduct in Battle

1.  To desert the ship or one's quarters in battle is punished with death or marooning.
2.  Good quarter to be given when craved.
3.  If any man shall defraud the company to the value of a dollar in plate, jewels, or money, marooning shall be his punishment.
4.  No man to talk of breaking up their way of living, till each had shared one thousand pounds sterling.

---

## VI. On Matters of Faith

### Option A (Naval Observance)
1.  Divine service in honour of the Divinity of Two shall be performed on board, and a sermon preached on the holy day, weather permitting.
2.  If any shall be heard to swear, curse, or blaspheme the name of The Two, he shall be punished as the Captain sees fit.

### Option B (Tolerant Company)
1.  Every man may worship as he sees fit, or not at all, so long as his practices do not endanger the ship or her company.
2.  No man shall be compelled to attend any religious service, nor shall any man be scorned for his private beliefs.

### Option C (No Gods Aboard)
1.  This ship shall be a haven from the squabbles of gods and priests. No open worship or proselytizing shall be permitted, for such things breed division.
2.  The only faith required is faith in the ship, the Captain, and the company.

---

## VII. Treatment of Outsiders

### Option A (The Pirate's Code)
1.  No boy or woman to be allowed amongst them. If any man be found seducing any of the latter sex, and carrying her to sea disguised, he is to suffer death.
2.  If at any time you meet with a prudent woman, that man that offers to meddle with her without her consent shall suffer present death.
3.  Prisoners are to be treated with fairness, yet any who attempts escape or incites mutiny amongst them shall be put to the sword.

### Option B (Naval Regulation)
1.  No passenger shall be taken aboard this vessel without the Captain's express consent. All who sail with us shall be subject to these articles.
2.  A fit place shall be set apart for the sick and hurt, prisoners included, where they may be tended to by the surgeon.
3.  The Commanders shall use their utmost endeavours to detect, apprehend, and bring to punishment all offenders, prisoners among them, who do violate the good order of the ship.

---

## VIII. On the Matter of Slavery

### Option A (Strict Abolition)
1.  This ship and her company shall take no part in the trade of human souls. No man, woman, or child shall be held in bondage aboard this vessel, whether as cargo or chattel. Any found engaged in such trafficking shall forfeit his share and be cast from the company.

### Option B (Cargo for Profit)
1.  Slaves may be taken as cargo from a prize, to be sold at the next port of call for the profit of the company. However, no slave shall be kept aboard for personal service, nor shall they be mistreated beyond what is necessary to secure them.

### Option C (The Buccaneer's Way)
1.  Slaves shall be considered a part of any prize and added to the common stock. They may be allotted as shares to the company, or given as compensation for grievous injury, as was the custom of the Brethren of the Coast. A man may receive a slave for the loss of a limb in lieu of coin, should he choose.

---

## IX. The Articles Themselves

1.  These articles shall be put in writing, by way of bond or obligation, and every man who joins the company shall set his hand to it, and be bound by its laws.
2.  He who offends against these articles shall be punished as they prescribe. All other faults and misdemeanors not herein mentioned shall be punished according to the laws and customs used at sea.
3.  These articles may be amended only by a majority vote of the company, and with the consent of the Captain and Quartermaster.
